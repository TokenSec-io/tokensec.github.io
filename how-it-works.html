How it works

How a blockchain register holds a security

A tokenised security is an ordinary share, note or unit whose ownership is recorded on a blockchain instead of in a spreadsheet or a registry database. The security is the same. The record of who owns it, and the way it moves, is what changes.

The parts

Four things work together. Only one of them is new.

The legal instrument
The company constitution, note terms or trust deed that creates the security and sets out the holder’s rights. This is unchanged by tokenisation.
The register
A program on the blockchain, often called a smart contract, that records how many units each holder owns. The issuer adopts it as its official register, or keeps it in step with one.
Verified holders
Each investor is identified and checked before they can hold units. The register only accepts transfers to verified, eligible holders.
Payment
Australian dollars, or a digital form of them, move between buyer and seller as the units change hands.

The life of a tokenised security

From issue to redemption, each step is recorded on the same register.

  1. Structure

    The issuer and its lawyers set the terms of the security, the offer documents and who may invest. The terms state that the blockchain record is the register.

  2. Verify investors

    Investors complete identity checks and confirm their eligibility, for example as wholesale investors. Their verified status is linked to their account on the register.

  3. Issue

    When the offer closes and funds clear, the register creates the units and records them against each investor. Each holder can see their holding immediately.

  4. Transfer

    When a holder sells, the register checks the buyer is eligible and that any transfer restrictions are met. Units and payment then change hands in the same step, so neither side waits on the other.

  5. Service

    Dividends, coupons and distributions are calculated from the register at the record date and paid to each holder. Notices and votes can reach holders through the same record.

  6. Redeem or retire

    When notes mature or units are bought back, holders are paid and the units are removed from the register. The full history stays on record.

The benefits

Most of the benefit comes from everyone working from one shared, current record.

For issuers

  • Issue securities in days, with lower registry and settlement costs.
  • Keep the register and cap table current, without reconciling separate records.
  • Reach more eligible investors by offering smaller parcels.
  • Pay dividends, coupons and distributions straight from the register.

For investors

  • Hold private shares and bonds from a smaller minimum, where the terms allow.
  • See your holding on the register as soon as it is issued or transferred.
  • Sell to another eligible investor, with settlement in minutes rather than days.
  • Check your holding and payment history yourself, at any time.

For advisers and partners

  • Audit a complete, time-stamped history of every issue, transfer and payment.
  • Rely on transfer rules that enforce eligibility and restrictions automatically.
  • Reduce the hand-offs between registries, custodians and payment providers.

What to keep in mind

The register makes securities easier to issue and move. It does not remove the risks of owning them.

Liquidity is not guaranteed
A faster transfer only helps if there is a buyer. Private shares and bonds may still be hard to sell before maturity.
The issuer’s risk is unchanged
If the business or asset performs poorly, a tokenised security loses value just as a paper one would.
Technology risk
Errors in the register program, or problems with the blockchain it runs on, could delay or disrupt transfers. Issuers should have the register code independently reviewed.
Access and recovery
Holders access their units through secure accounts. The terms should set out how a holding is restored if access is lost.
Law and regulation
The offer still needs the right licence, disclosure documents and investor checks. The terms must make clear how the blockchain record relates to the legal register.
